Kenal sambal · what is sambal

Chilli, pounded with whatever the island grows

Sambal is a chilli relish ground in a cobek, a shallow stone mortar, with an ulekan pestle. The chilli is a constant. Everything else changes from island to island: citrus in Sulawesi, smoked fish in Manado, peanuts in East Java, fermented durian in Jambi. Most sambals fall into three families, and the same three colours mark them on the map. Tap one.

Chilli is a newcomer

Chillies come from the Americas and reached the archipelago through Portuguese and Spanish trade in the 1500s. Before then, heat came from native long pepper, cabe jawa.

Java has the most

Both major surveys find that Java, the island that grows the most chilli, has the largest share of sambal varieties, followed by Sumatra.

The mortar matters

Pounding crushes cells and releases oils that a blender chops past. A cobek also lets you leave texture: rough for raw sambal, smooth for cooked.

The Spice Islands came first

Long before chilli arrived, cloves from North Maluku and nutmeg from the Banda Islands drew Arab, Chinese, Portuguese and Dutch ships to the archipelago. Rempah built the trade routes that later carried chilli in.

Padanan · what are you eating?

Name a dish, get three sambals

Any cuisine works. Claude picks three sambals from the profiled ones in this atlas and says why each one suits it.

Cut the richness

Coconut curries, rendang and fried food want acid. Raw sambals with lime, tomato or unripe fruit, like dabu-dabu, acan or lado mudo, clean the palate.

Echo the fire

Grilled fish and meat suit smoky or toasted sambals. Roa and cakalang bring smoked fish; terasi brings toasted shrimp.

Fresh with fresh

Seafood and salads go with sliced, uncooked sambals like matah and colo-colo, which add aroma without weight.

Sweet calms the heat

Cooked sambals with palm sugar or peanuts, such as bajak and pecel, are gentler. They suit children, vegetables and anyone new to chilli.
